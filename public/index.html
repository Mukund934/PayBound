Bounded authority for autonomous commerce

An agent may interpret a refund.
It may not decide what money moves.

A refund is the only ordinary e-commerce operation that moves money out of a merchant account — and the one an agent is most likely to be asked to perform from a paragraph a stranger wrote. Everything downstream of that paragraph is a payments problem, not a prompt problem. So PayBound is the boundary, not the classifier.

Why no live model here

by design

The router is a real Gemini call in the benchmark — it is how every committed trial was routed. It is not wired to this page. The free tier is 20 requests a day and it is this project's binding constraint, so a public endpoint calling it would exhaust the benchmark on the first curious visitor and show quota errors to everyone after.

A free-text box here would also be a second, weaker router running without the corpus seal or provenance stamp — output that looks like a measurement while being none.

Execution endpoint

…

POST /api/execute needs two independent switches, a bearer token compared in constant time, and an explicit non-dry body. It refuses any request naming an amount rather than ignoring the field.

On a serverless host the write-ahead intent ledger does not outlive the invocation, so at-most-once rests on the Razorpay read-back alone. Live execution there requires a third, explicit acknowledgement of that. The recorded demonstration runs on a machine where the ledger is a real file.
